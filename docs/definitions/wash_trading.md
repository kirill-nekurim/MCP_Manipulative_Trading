# Wash Trading

## Definition

Wash trading is an illegal market manipulation practice in which the same trader, or colluding parties, simultaneously buy and sell the same security to create the appearance of legitimate market activity.

The purpose of wash trading is to generate misleading information about trading volume, liquidity, or market interest in a financial asset.

Unlike legitimate trading, wash trades do not result in a meaningful change in beneficial ownership.

## Main Characteristics

Key characteristics of wash trading include:

* Simultaneous or near-simultaneous buy and sell transactions.
* No meaningful economic transfer of ownership.
* Artificial inflation of trading volume.
* Creation of misleading market signals.
* Potential use in broader market manipulation schemes.

## Objectives of Wash Trading

Market participants may engage in wash trading to:

* Inflate reported trading volume.
* Create the appearance of market interest.
* Attract additional investors.
* Manipulate market prices.
* Support pump-and-dump schemes.
* Generate exchange incentives or trading rebates.

## How Wash Trading Works

A typical wash trading scheme follows these steps:

1. A trader places buy and sell orders for the same asset.
2. The orders execute against each other directly or through coordinated counterparties.
3. Trading volume increases without meaningful ownership change.
4. Other market participants observe the increased activity.
5. Investors may interpret the volume as evidence of genuine demand.
6. The manipulator benefits from subsequent market reactions.

## Example

A trader and a cooperating broker repeatedly buy and sell shares of a stock.

The resulting transactions create the appearance of heavy trading activity.

Other investors notice the unusual volume and begin purchasing the stock.

After the price rises, the manipulator sells legitimate holdings for a profit.

## Regulatory Status

### United States

Wash trading is prohibited under U.S. securities and commodities regulations.

Relevant regulators include:

* Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)
* Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC)
* Internal Revenue Service (IRS)

### Commodity Exchange Act

The Commodity Exchange Act prohibits wash trading in regulated commodity markets.

The legislation was introduced to prevent market participants from creating false impressions of trading activity.

### IRS Wash Sale Rule

The IRS applies a separate concept known as a wash sale.

A wash sale occurs when:

* A security is sold at a loss.
* The same or substantially identical security is repurchased within 30 days.

Losses generated through wash sales generally cannot be deducted for tax purposes.

## Market Impact

Wash trading can negatively affect financial markets by:

* Distorting trading volume statistics.
* Misleading investors.
* Obscuring true market liquidity.
* Reducing market transparency.
* Undermining confidence in market data.

## Relationship with High-Frequency Trading

The rise of high-frequency trading (HFT) increased regulatory concern regarding wash trading.

High-frequency trading systems can execute thousands of trades per second using automated algorithms and low-latency infrastructure.

Regulators have investigated whether certain HFT strategies could facilitate wash trading activity.

### Regulatory Example

In 2014, the SEC charged Wedbush Securities with failing to maintain sufficient controls over customer trading systems.

According to regulators, these deficiencies allowed certain customers to engage in wash trading and other manipulative practices.

## Wash Trading in Cryptocurrency Markets

Wash trading is considered a significant issue in cryptocurrency markets.

Several factors contribute to its prevalence:

* Limited regulatory oversight in some jurisdictions.
* Fragmented market structure.
* Inconsistent reporting standards.
* Incentives to attract traders through inflated volume statistics.

### Artificial Volume Inflation

Crypto exchanges or traders may engage in wash trading to make a token appear more liquid and popular than it actually is.

Artificially inflated volume may:

* Increase investor interest.
* Improve exchange rankings.
* Attract new market participants.

### Research Findings

A 2022 Forbes analysis of 157 cryptocurrency exchanges estimated that more than half of reported Bitcoin trading volume could be fake or associated with non-economic wash trading activity.

### Connection to Pump-and-Dump Schemes

Wash trading is frequently associated with pump-and-dump schemes.

The manipulator may:

1. Generate artificial trading activity.
2. Promote the asset to attract investors.
3. Drive prices higher.
4. Sell holdings at inflated prices.

## Historical Cases

### LIBOR Manipulation Scandal

Wash trades were reportedly used in connection with the LIBOR manipulation scandal.

According to regulatory findings, traders executed wash trades through a brokerage firm and generated commissions that served as compensation for assistance in manipulating benchmark interest rate submissions.

### Stock Manipulation Example

A trader and brokerage firm may coordinate rapid purchases and sales of a stock.

The resulting volume attracts attention from other investors.

Once the price rises due to increased interest, the manipulator profits from selling previously acquired positions.

## Detection Methods

Regulators and exchanges monitor for indicators such as:

* Matching buy and sell orders.
* Repeated transactions between related accounts.
* Unusual trading volume patterns.
* Lack of meaningful position changes.
* Suspiciously high turnover rates.

## Why Wash Trading Is Harmful

Wash trading harms market integrity because it creates false signals about:

* Liquidity.
* Investor demand.
* Market sentiment.
* Price discovery.

Investors relying on manipulated data may make poor investment decisions and suffer financial losses.
